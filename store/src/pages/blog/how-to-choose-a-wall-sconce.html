How to choose a wall sconce, room by room

Wall sconces are the quiet overachievers of lighting: they free up floor and table space, flatter everything they touch, and make a ceiling feel taller. Here's how to pick the right one for each room — the same questions we ask before adding any piece to our collection.

The four questions

Before you look at a single product, answer these:

  • What job does it do? Ambient glow, task light, or accent? Most rooms want a mix across fixtures, not everything from one.
  • Which direction should light travel? Up light makes ceilings feel taller; down light pools on task areas; up-down grazing shows off texture.
  • What size fits? A sconce should relate to what it's beside — a door, a bed, a mirror — not fight it.
  • Where does power come from? Hardwired, plug-in, or battery? This decides your shortlist faster than style does.

Entryway

Mount at roughly eye level — 66–72 in (168–183 cm) to the centre of the fixture — either side of the door or mirror. Up-down designs earn their keep here: they mark the entry and make hardworking walls beautiful.

Living room

Sconces shine where lamps can't live: flanking a sofa when the side table is full of everything else, or washing art without the floor footprint of a spot. Pair with dimmable warm lamps and let the ceiling lights rest.

Bedroom

Bedside sconces free the nightstand and — aimed at the page, not the pillow — are kinder to a sleeping partner than any table lamp. Mount around 30–36 in (76–91 cm) above the mattress top, or choose an adjustable arm.

Bathroom (and powder rooms)

Light the face, not the floor: sconces at eye level either side of the mirror beat any single overhead fixture. Use fittings rated for humid zones and keep 2700–3000 K for a kind mirror.

Outdoors

This is where ratings matter. IP65 means dust-tight and protected against water jets — right for exposed walls in normal weather. Near the coast, favour powder-coated aluminum and rinse fixtures occasionally. Our full explainer lives in the FAQ.

Sizing rule of thumb: a sconce beside a door should be roughly ¼ to ⅓ the door's height. Beside a bed, 20–30% of the headboard width.

Colour temperature, once and for all

2700–3000 K = warm white — living rooms, bedrooms, entries, anywhere you unwind. 3500–4000 K is a cool neutral for workspaces. Above that is commercial. If a listing doesn't tell you the temperature, be suspicious of the rest of its numbers too.

The one-minute checklist

  • Job, direction, size, power — in that order
  • Eye-level mounting for entries and mirrors
  • IP65+ for exposed outdoor walls
  • 3000 K or below anywhere you relax
  • Published specs and a real warranty on the listing — always
